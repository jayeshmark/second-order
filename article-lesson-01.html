UGC for Products — Lesson 1

This series teaches UGC marketing for your product, in order, from zero. This lesson is the start.

Your influencer posts were never UGC

Most product owners meet UGC like this.

They pay a few influencers to post about their product. The posts look good. The sales do not come. And they walk away saying UGC does not work for us.

But that was never UGC. That was an ad wearing a person's face.

An influencer post is you renting someone's audience and reading your pitch in their voice. UGC is a different animal entirely.

What UGC actually is

UGC is content your brand never makes.

Creators and customers make it. It is filmed on phones, in bedrooms and kitchens and cars, and it looks like everything else in the feed, because it is made the way everything else in the feed is made.

Your brand's job is not to make it. Your brand's job is to get the right people making it, about your product, again and again.

That one shift changes everything: what you pay for, who you hire, and how you judge the results.

Why it works when ads do not

Three reasons, and they stack.

A person is believed. An ad is not. Someone using your product on camera, in their own words, gets trust your polished ad will never get. People can smell an ad in half a second. They lean in for a person.

Volume beats polish. Twenty creator videos cost less than one polished ad shoot. One expensive ad is one guess. Twenty cheap videos are twenty guesses, and the feed tells you which ones were right.

The feed rewards native content. Platforms push what looks like the platform. Content made by creators, in the feed's own language, gets shown. Content that looks like a commercial gets skipped, and the algorithm learns to skip it faster.

The four kinds of people who make it

Every UGC program is built from some mix of these four. They are not the same job, and paying for the wrong one is where most budgets die.

Creators. Paid to film. No audience needed. You are buying the content itself: their face, their voice, their filming skill. This is the backbone of most programs.

Influencers. A rented audience. You are buying reach into people who already follow them. Useful for a push, dangerous as a foundation, because the audience leaves when the post does.

Affiliates. Paid when it sells. No sale, no pay. You are buying results, so the good ones pick products they believe will move.

Customers. Real users. Nobody pays them, or they get small perks. They are believed most of all, because they have no reason to lie. You cannot hire them. You can only earn them, and make it easy for them to post.

When someone says we tried UGC and it flopped, they usually bought influencers and called it UGC. Different tool, different job.

Keep the humans real

One shortcut will tempt you, so take it off the table now.

AI can generate UGC style videos today, faces and voices included. It is cheap and it is fast. It also does not sell.

SideShift, a platform that runs creator campaigns for apps, has run hundreds of them. Across those campaigns, AI UGC did not convert next to human UGC.

The reason is the whole lesson in one line. UGC works because a real person is believed. A generated face spends the trust instead of building it.

Use AI behind the camera if you want: research, briefs, editing. Keep the human in front of it real.

The fork: what you sell changes what UGC looks like

UGC is one idea, but it changes shape with your product. Know your fork before you copy anyone's playbook.

If you sell an app, your product demos itself on camera. The creator opens it, uses it, shows the result. The screen is the proof.

If you sell SaaS, nobody wants to watch a dashboard. Your product sells a workflow and an outcome: the painful before, the tool in the middle, the finished result on screen. The creator films the change, not the features.

If you sell a physical product, the proof is in the hand. Unbox it, hold it, use it in real life. Touch is the argument.

Same system, three shapes. Most UGC advice online quietly assumes one fork, usually apps. If yours is a different one, translate before you copy.

The honest test: does UGC fit your product at all

UGC is not for everyone, and pretending otherwise wastes your money.

It fits best when the buyer is a person buying for themselves: consumer apps people subscribe to, tools built for creators and small teams, physical products people buy online.

It can work, with changes, for products sold to small businesses, where a credible expert creator carries the proof.

And it will not carry these: enterprise products sold to committees, luxury products, regulated products, and anything with nothing to show on camera. If your product has no moment a stranger can film, UGC has nothing to hold.

Three questions tell you where you stand:

  1. Can your product show its win on camera in a few seconds?
  2. Is your buyer scrolling a feed, buying for themselves, able to act on the spot?
  3. Is one new customer worth enough that many cheap videos make sense?

Three yes answers: this series is built for you. A no on the first one is the hard stop. The rest can be worked around.

The deeper point

Ads rent attention. The price is set by auction, it rises every year, and the day you stop paying, the attention stops.

UGC builds something you keep: people who know how to sell your product on camera, formats that keep working, and proof filmed by strangers instead of claims made by you.

That is why this is a system and not a stunt. And a system needs its parts in order.
